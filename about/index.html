WHY GAMES

Curiosity began with playing.

As a child, I loved playing Mario on the Famicom. A book tracing the Mario series and Nintendo’s development made me curious about the people and decisions behind those games; I read it several times.

I began programming in middle school and enjoyed making a function work. A Scratch competition with a classmate was an early step toward making games. That interest led me to Digital Media Technology at Hangzhou Dianzi University, where I chose game-focused courses and joined development competitions.

02 / EXPERIENCE

MY DESIGN INTEREST

Design for the player’s learning.

I am especially interested in Soulslike action games and the way players learn through repeated attempts. Becoming familiar with controls and enemy patterns can turn a difficult encounter into a felt achievement.

That interest led me to break down and implement poise, impact, input buffering and action cancellation. I want to connect the engineering of those systems to the experience they create.

AI DEVELOPMENT JOURNEY

My AI practice changed in stages.

I did not begin with AI writing whole systems. My role changed as I learned where a model could help and where my own structure and judgment were still essential.

BEFORE 2025

Ask and research

I used AI mainly like a technical search partner: comparing approaches, clarifying unfamiliar concepts and checking possible solutions.

JUN 2025

Complete the code beside me

With AI completion in VS Code, I began asking code-level questions while keeping implementation under direct control.

DEC 2025

Let the tool execute

TRAE and Kiro made direct code generation practical. Hooks, Agents.md and compile checks became ways to constrain and inspect the result.

MAR 2026

Work in bounded slices

I learned to split a design into plans, have AI implement one bounded part at a time, then integrate, play and correct it. CLI and MCP tools extended the workflow into UI and debugging.

JUN 2026

Extend it across the framework

I expanded the approach to the whole game framework, building a shared AI knowledge base and reusable skills for each stage of development, then sharing the practice with teammates.

THE PRINCIPLE

A workflow has to recover from its own mistakes.

AI output is a draft, not a result. My workflow keeps a human decision at both ends and makes failure visible in the middle.

01

Clarify

Define the player goal and constraints.

02

Plan

Split the work into a bounded change.

03

Generate

Ask the tool to implement one part.

04

Check

Compile and inspect Unity console output.

05

Repair

Use failures to guide the next change.

06

Play

Judge the feel and fold the lesson back in.

ENGINEERING AND LEARNING

Build the foundation, then extend it.

I built my technical foundation through C#, C++ and Unity projects before adopting generative AI in development. Little Knight was independently designed and programmed; in the original collaborative Air Command I designed the card battles and implemented movement and combat.

Game-client development is currently my strongest skill. Production work has also taught me to clarify ambiguous requirements, coordinate with design and art, and verify features within a larger team’s process.

In a university graphics assignment, writing a cloth simulation and a Phong shader was difficult for me. I studied a Unity shader book and supplemented my graphics knowledge with GAMES101 before completing the assignment. That experience made the gaps in my understanding concrete and gave me a way to work through them.

WHAT I WANT TO EXPLORE

Grow toward technical design.

I want to develop as a technical designer who can connect gameplay intent, implementation and player experience. I am drawn to multiplayer action RPGs in which players identify with the roles they take on.

Working alongside UI/UX and art colleagues has made me more attentive to visual design. Production reviews also introduced me to retention and other player data, and to how teams use evidence to plan the next version. I want a stronger foundation in design methods, evaluation, graphics and the management of a complete game project.

In the longer term, I hope to take broader responsibility for a game’s direction, drawing on technical understanding and experience across the development process.
