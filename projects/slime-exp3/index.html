PROJECT 04

Slime Wants to EXP+3!

Collection becomes competition when players can steal a resource or interrupt the other player.

48-hour G-Bits university game jam · 2023Unity · C#Four-person team · Second prize
Play in browser
Local two-player gamePlay together on one keyboard. The in-game instructions explain each player’s controls.
MY ROLE

Team lead, gameplay design and shared programming; resource collection and snatching.

DESIGN FOCUS

Make collecting a resource create an opportunity for interference.

WHAT TO INSPECT

Browser-playable two-player game, original rules and a 48-hour team decision.

CORE LOOP

The same droplet creates two decisions.

Two slimes use tentacles to pull in water droplets, gain experience and choose upgrades. Pulling takes time. An opponent can contest the resource or cut a tentacle, so each attempt to collect creates an opportunity for interference.

COLLECT

Commit to a resource

Choose a droplet and spend time pulling it in.

CONTEST

Steal or interrupt

Challenge the other player’s collection attempt.

ADAPT

Choose an upgrade

Change capacity or behavior as experience grows.

GAMEPLAY DESIGN

Collection has a cost. Interruption has a reward.

The original rule panels explain the choices inside the core loop. The English notes below unpack each panel; the artwork and diagrams are retained from the team’s game instructions.

01 / POSITION AND COMMIT

Aim for a shared resource.

Move along your side and launch a tentacle toward a droplet. Collection requires reaching the resource and bringing it back, giving the other player time to respond.

The opening action establishes the competition: both players act on the same pool of resources.

02 / REWARD AND EXPOSURE

More water, more to lose.

Collected water takes time to digest, and a larger stored amount improves digestion efficiency. Cutting the opponent’s tentacle stuns them and releases their undigested water back into the field.

This creates a risk-and-reward choice: keep collecting for efficiency, or interfere before the opponent secures the gain.

03 / CHANGE THE NEXT EXCHANGE

Choose how to grow.

Levelling up pauses play for a choice of greater collection power, additional tentacles or a random skill. These choices change how the next collection attempt or confrontation can unfold.

The original instructions set a 250-point victory goal, giving the competition a clear ending.

MY PROGRAMMING CONTRIBUTION

Turn the tentacle into a complete interaction.

I implemented the resource-collection and snatching functions. The retained TentacleManager code checks the game’s pause state, updates tentacle extension or retraction, keeps the endpoint aligned and transfers a carried droplet to the slime when the tentacle has fully returned.

This connects several parts of the experience: the visible reach, the time spent retrieving a resource, and the moment collection becomes available to the player. The wider gameplay design and programming were shared with the team.

TEAM LEADERSHIP

Choose a scope the team can finish.

Three ideas, one 48-hour commitment.

What I noticed

The jam theme was Absorption. During brainstorming, our team spent time debating three possible directions, and we did not yet know how much we could complete.

What I did

I compared the design and implementation advantages of each idea against the deadline and our team’s abilities, then asked the team to vote. We chose the slime concept, inspired by Gold Miner, and prioritized a complete playable loop before expanding it.

What I learned

As team lead, I recruited teammates, organized the discussion, allocated work and tracked progress. Our team received second prize. The experience taught me to use a finished core as the basis for deciding what to add next.

COLLABORATION

My contribution in a small team.

I coordinated the project and participated in gameplay design, programming and animation. I implemented resource collection and stealing. Planning was shared across the team, and programming was shared with Xu Chengchi.

Our four-person team included Xu Chengchi, Wang Sijie, Zheng Jiayu and me. Programming was shared with Xu Chengchi; art was by Wang Sijie and Zheng Jiayu, with audio by Xu Chengchi and shared animation contributions.

The 48-hour format made scope a design decision. The key was to get the competitive collection interaction working as a complete loop, with enough feedback for two players to understand it.
