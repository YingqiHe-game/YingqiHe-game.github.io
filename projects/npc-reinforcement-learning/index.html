What I would investigate next

The project focused mainly on combat strength. It did not establish a range of distinctive NPC styles. Future evaluation should compare behavior diversity, robustness against fixed opponents and the experience of playing against the agent.

RESEARCH QUESTION

How do incentives become behavior?

I investigated reinforcement learning for fighting-game NPCs in my graduation project at Hangzhou Dianzi University. I first used FightingICE and Dueling DQN to study reward design, then applied that environment-design experience to a custom Unity training setup.

The project connects a research question to game engineering: what does the agent observe, which actions can it take, and which behavior does the reward actually encourage?

ENVIRONMENT AND TRAINING PIPELINE

Two environments for two stages of the work.

In FightingICE, I connected the training process to a fighting-game environment and used combat outcomes to evaluate policy behavior. The platform let me work on algorithm and reward choices before building a larger 3D environment.

In Unity, I built the modular fighting system and exposed it through ML-Agents. The environments are separate: the 2D work informed the design of the 3D training setup, rather than transferring one trained network directly between them.

2D REWARD DESIGN

Change what the reward tells the agent.

The baseline 2D reward used the difference between damage dealt and damage received. The revised version separates attack and defense weighting, scales the health terms, and adds distance and time terms.

r = opponent_HP_loss − self_HP_loss

REVISED

OBSERVATION AND ACTION DESIGN

A compact representation of the combat state.

My final Unity observation design uses 15 scalar values. I chose health, stamina, animation state, position and orientation instead of feeding the full game image into the policy.

Actions map to the player’s control vocabulary.

I kept the camera locked onto the opponent so the policy could focus on combat rather than learning camera control. Seven discrete action branches map to the existing character-input interface.

Read reward signs together with the delta definition.

In the 3D code, HP deltas are current minus previous values. A positive coefficient on self HP change makes received damage negative reward; a negative coefficient on opponent HP change makes damage dealt positive reward.

ITERATION AND SELF-PLAY

A reward can encourage the wrong shortcut.

A combination of time and distance penalties encouraged the agent to approach quickly and trade attacks to finish the fight. That behavior reduced penalties, but did not fully match the tactical behavior I wanted.

I changed the relative reward weights over training, placing less emphasis on distance and time and more on health outcomes. This became a practical lesson: a better reward number is not automatically a better NPC.

Self-play provides changing opponents. Reward and Elo therefore describe different aspects of the run, and these charts cover different step ranges. I use them alongside observed behavior rather than as interchangeable proof.
