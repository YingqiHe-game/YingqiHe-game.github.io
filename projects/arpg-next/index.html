PARRY, TIMELINE AND WEAPONS

Two demonstrations, two different systems.

How parry and weapon switching work

Parry creates a stateful opportunity.

An animation-timed parry window sets isParryEnabled. A hit during that window puts the attacker into parry stun; the execution interaction becomes active while that state is present. If the player attacks within the interaction range, I bind the attacker and victim to Timeline tracks.

Timeline aligns the actors, locks their actions during the sequence, applies weapon damage multiplied by its execution rate, and releases the locks through completion callbacks. It is a gameplay state transition with coordinated presentation.

Changing a weapon changes its behavior.

WeaponFactory replaces the equipped prefab and loads its data. WeaponManager refreshes the colliders and uses AnimatorOverrideController to substitute the three attack clips for that weapon type. The impact class can start from the weapon type and be overridden for an individual attack.

That is why the heavy-weapon clip shows a new moveset and a different reaction. Poise damage governs the interruption threshold; impact selects the reaction that supplies the visual weight and displacement.

RootMotionController forwards Animator.deltaPosition to the actor, and ActorController applies the accumulated movement to the Rigidbody in FixedUpdate. The selected reaction animation therefore connects the impact class to visible displacement.

Soulslike action games, including Elden Ring, drew me to combat design. What interests me is the way repeated attempts let the player learn the controls and recognize an enemy’s moves. Overcoming the encounter can make that learning feel tangible.

COMBAT DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION

Combat is more than a damage number.

I designed the combat around separate questions: does a hit remove health, does it interrupt the target, and what reaction should that interruption produce? Separating these lets weapons differ in timing, commitment and control of an encounter.

Three separate parts of the normal-hit response
ParameterImplementationDesign purpose
Health damageWeaponData.ATKPhysicalReduce the target’s HP.
Poise damageWeaponData.poiseDamageDetermine when a hit interrupts an action.
Impact classWeaponController.currentImpactSelect the hit-reaction animation.

A target can lose health while retaining enough poise to continue its action. Once its poise breaks, the impact class determines the visible response. The stronger knockback in the heavy-weapon demonstration comes through that reaction and animation system.

THRESHOLDS AND TRADE-OFFS

Poise makes interruption a threshold decision.

In the prototype, the default poise pool is 40. An unblocked, unparried hit subtracts the weapon’s poise damage. Reaching zero triggers hit stun and restores the poise pool. Further poise damage restarts a recovery timer; after five seconds without another poise-damaging hit, the pool is restored.

In HitStun, I select a reaction clip from the weapon’s impact class through AnimatorOverrideController, disable active weapon colliders, and clear pending roll and attack triggers. This connects the threshold decision to an actual interruption.

// Condensed from the normal-hit branch.
if (statusManager.AddPoise(-weapon.GetPoiseDamage()))
    HitStun(weapon.currentImpact);

statusManager.AddHP(-weapon.GetATK());

Commitment also affects resistance.

When an attack’s weapon window activates, the prototype adds half of that weapon’s poise damage to the attacker’s own poise and spends stamina. That connects offensive commitment with resistance to interruption. The heavier weapon costs more stamina and grants a larger bonus.

LAYERS, OVERRIDES AND TIMELINE

Move and guard without duplicating every motion.

Two layers, two responsibilities.

The original controller has a Base Layer for locomotion and full-body actions, plus a defense layer with an upperBody Avatar Mask. The base can continue movement while the masked layer supplies the shield pose. This lets movement and guarding combine without a separate full-body clip for every movement direction.

ActorController sets the defense layer weight to 1 when the player holds a shield and defending is allowed. It clears the layer when defense is disallowed, on block stun, or on the defense-idle callback after the player releases guard. The implementation uses direct weight changes alongside Animator transitions.

Reuse the state machine, change the moveset.

WeaponManager replaces the three attack slots through AnimatorOverrideController, indexed by weapon type. The attack chain and its conditions remain in the controller while the clips change with the equipped weapon. Hit reactions use another override: the impact class selects the clip for the hit slot.

State-machine behaviors such as FSMOnEnter and FSMOnExit forward configured callbacks to the actor. Attack entry locks movement, and exit restores control. Configured callbacks connect the action states to weapon and control changes. In this prototype, combat control and animation timing are closely connected; the later skill tables make those intervals more explicit and easier to inspect.

Coordinate two actors with Timeline.

DirectorController binds the Attacker Animation and Victim Animation tracks to the two actors. At execution start it aligns their facing and position, locks their actions and rotation, disables weapon colliders and grants temporary invulnerability. The hit callback applies ATK × executionRate; separate completion callbacks restore each actor’s controls and remove invulnerability.

This is distinct from the ordinary attack chain: the execution needs synchronized presentation and state changes for two participants.

Make reaction motion reach the character.

RootMotionController forwards Animator.deltaPosition to the actor. ActorController accumulates that displacement and applies it through the Rigidbody in FixedUpdate. The selected hit-reaction animation can therefore create visible movement as well as a different pose.

See how the interruption becomes a reaction.

The impact value indexes a hit-animation clip, which replaces the hit slot through AnimatorOverrideController. The method then disables both weapons and clears roll and light-attack triggers before triggering the reaction.

This is the implementation behind the distinction above: poise decides whether the hit interrupts, while impact chooses how that interruption looks and moves the actor.

These boundaries expose the trade-off between commitment and responsiveness.

GAME FEEL AS CONFIGURATION

Tune commitment and responsiveness as data.

In the skill-system extension, I turn steering, movement and follow-up timing into timeline actions. Rather than giving every move the same response rules, I can tune the interval for which each constraint applies.

Skill configuration as a set of feel-tuning decisions
ControlWhat the runtime gatesWhat I can tune
LOCK_ROTATIONDirection updates during an active windowHow late the player can retarget a swing.
LOCK_MOVEMENTMovement input during an active windowHow committed or mobile an attack feels.
OPEN_QUEUE_WINDOWAccepting the next skill as buffered inputHow forgiving follow-up timing feels.
OPEN_CANCEL_WINDOWReplacing or cancelling the active skillWhen the player can leave the current commitment.

The runtime checks these active actions. A follow-up inside a cancel window can replace the current skill; during a queue window it can be buffered; outside those windows it is rejected. Cancellation also removes owned hit-volume runtime objects.

Moving these boundaries changes player control while keeping the animation and damage concept intact. This gives me an explicit way to iterate on combat feel, instead of hiding every timing decision inside one animation controller.
